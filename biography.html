Biography

From a Vermont bedroom to Stick Season

Early Life

Noah Kahan was born in 1997 and raised in Strafford, Vermont, a small rural town he has described as central to both his identity and his songwriting. Music was part of his household growing up, and he began writing songs and posting them online as a teenager, building a following on platforms like YouTube and SoundCloud before he had a record deal.

Getting Discovered

Kahan’s early single “Young Blood” caught the attention of Republic Records, leading to a record deal while he was still a teenager. He spent his late teens and early twenties developing his sound, releasing music that leaned closer to conventional pop before returning to the folk and acoustic influences of his childhood.

Breaking Through

“Stick season, it’s already got its icy grip on me” — the opening line that launched a thousand playlists.

His 2021 EP Cape Elizabeth signaled a turn toward the introspective, New England-rooted sound that would define his career. That shift crystallized with the 2022 release of his third studio album, Stick Season, named for the bleak stretch of late autumn in northern New England after the leaves have fallen and before the snow arrives. The album — and its title track in particular — went viral on TikTok and streaming platforms, resonating with listeners far outside Vermont who connected with its themes of homesickness, arrested development, and small-town restlessness.

The Stick Season Era

Kahan followed the album with deluxe reissues (Stick Season (We’ll All Be Here Forever) and Stick Season (Forever)), packed with additional tracks and high-profile collaborations, including “Northern Attitude” with Zach Bryan and “Call Your Mom” with Lizzy McAlpine. The extended rollout kept the project in the cultural conversation for nearly two years, moving him from small clubs to headlining arenas and festivals worldwide.

Timeline

1997 — Born in Vermont.

Teens — Signs with Republic Records after early singles gain attention online.

2019 — Releases debut album Busyhead.

2021 — Releases I Was / I Am and the Cape Elizabeth EP, moving toward a folk-driven sound.

2022 — Releases Stick Season, his commercial breakthrough.

2023 — Releases deluxe editions We’ll All Be Here Forever and Forever; launches The Busyhead Project.

2024 — Nominated for a Grammy Award for Best New Artist; headlines major festivals and arena tours.
